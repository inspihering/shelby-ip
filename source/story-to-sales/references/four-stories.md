# The 4 Expert Stories — Full Guidance

For each story: purpose, the questions to ask the user, what to avoid, and the output format to draft toward. Use the questions conversationally — one or two at a time, not as a questionnaire dump — and only ask what the story genuinely needs.

## 1. Origin Story

**Purpose:** Explain where the work began and why it matters to the expert personally or professionally.

**Ask:**
- Where did this work begin for you?
- What problem did you keep seeing?
- What experience shaped your perspective?
- What did you notice that others were missing?
- Why does this work matter to you personally or professionally?
- What part of your background gives emotional depth to your brand?

**Avoid:** Turning this into a full life story. Focus on the specific experiences that led directly to the current work — not a comprehensive biography.

**Output format:**
> Before I became known for [expertise], I came from [background/experience]. What I kept noticing was [problem/tension]. That experience led me to believe [core insight], which is why I now help [audience] with [transformation].

**Best used for:** Keynote opening, about page, podcast interviews, brand videos, long-form social posts, speaker introductions.

---

## 2. Credibility Story

**Purpose:** Show what makes the expert uniquely qualified — authority without sounding like a résumé.

**Ask:**
- What have you studied, practiced, built, led, overcome, or achieved?
- What gives you authority in this space?
- What results have you created?
- What patterns have you seen repeatedly, across which audience or industry?
- What unique combination of experience do you bring?
- What makes your perspective different from others in your field?
- What have you done enough times that you can now teach it, package it, or systematize it?

**Avoid:** Generic authority claims ("years of experience," "proven track record") that could apply to anyone. Translate experience into market relevance — what does this credibility mean for the person trying to decide whether to trust this expert?

**Output format:**
> My expertise comes from [experience/credential/body of work]. Over time, I saw [pattern/problem] repeated across [audience/industry/context]. That allowed me to develop [framework/process/approach], which helps [audience] achieve [specific result].

**Best used for:** Speaker one-sheet, sales page, proposal, bio, media kit, LinkedIn profile, event planner pitch.

---

## 3. Transformation Story

**Purpose:** Show what the expert personally walked through, and what they now know because of it — this is what builds emotional connection and trust.

**Ask:**
- What challenge, transition, failure, reinvention, or growth process shaped you?
- What did you have to learn the hard way?
- What belief changed for you?
- What did you have to let go of?
- What did you discover about yourself, your work, or your audience?
- How did that transformation shape your current framework?
- What lesson from your transformation is useful to your audience specifically?

**Avoid:** Oversharing or centering trauma unnecessarily. This story should connect personal experience to professional wisdom, not maximize vulnerability for its own sake. If the user offers something that feels like more disclosure than the deliverable needs, it's fine to reflect that back and ask how much they actually want to share publicly.

**Output format:**
> There was a point when I [challenge/turning point]. At first, I believed [old belief]. Through that experience, I learned [new insight]. That shift changed how I [lead/teach/sell/speak/build/serve], and now I help others make that same kind of transformation through [framework/process].

**Best used for:** Keynote middle section, brand story, sales conversations, podcast interviews, email nurture sequences, social media storytelling.

---

## 4. Client Story

**Purpose:** Prove the framework works for someone other than the expert — evidence, not just personal philosophy.

**Ask:**
- Who did you help?
- What problem were they facing?
- What was at stake for them?
- What part of your framework did you use?
- What changed for them?
- What tangible result did they achieve?
- What intangible shift happened?
- What does this story prove about your work?

**Avoid:** Inventing or embellishing clients, results, or outcomes. If the user doesn't have a specific detail (a number, a name, a quote), ask for it or draft with a placeholder rather than making one up. Keep it specific enough to build credibility but flexible enough to protect confidentiality — first name only, "a client in [industry]," or fully anonymized are all fine if that's what's available.

**Output format:**
> One client came to me with [problem]. They were struggling with [specific challenge]. We applied [framework/process/step], and the shift was [change]. As a result, they achieved [result/transformation]. This story shows that [core belief/proof point].

**Best used for:** Sales calls, offer pages, case studies, testimonials, proposals, webinar teaching, closing section of a keynote.

---

## Story Evaluation Criteria

Before finalizing any story, check it against these:

- Is it relevant to the audience the expert is trying to reach?
- Does it support the expert's authority?
- Does it connect to a problem the audience actually cares about?
- Does it reveal a useful insight, not just an anecdote?
- Does it create emotional connection?
- Does it point toward the expert's framework?
- Does it make the expert's work easier to understand?
- Does it build trust?
- Does it make the offer easier to buy or refer?
- Is it focused enough to actually use in business messaging — not a meandering life story?

If a draft fails several of these, it needs another pass before it's ready to use.
