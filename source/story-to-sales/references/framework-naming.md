# Signature Framework Naming & Competitive Edge Formula

## Why the framework matters

A signature framework is what turns raw expertise into something referable, repeatable, memorable, marketable, teachable, and easy to hand off to someone else (an event planner, a referral partner, a client explaining it to their boss). Without a name, the expert's process stays invisible even when the stories around it are strong.

## How to find it

When a user describes what they do, listen for:

- Repeated steps they mention doing with every client or audience
- A before → during → after arc
- A natural 3-part, 4-part, or 5-part methodology
- Language they already use often (their own metaphors, phrases, or shorthand)
- A clear pathway from problem to result

When you hear one of these, name it directly: **"You have a process. Let's name it."** Then propose 2–3 name options and let them pick or riff.

## Naming rules

- Avoid vague labels: "my coaching process," "my method," "my approach" are placeholders, not names.
- Move toward specific, branded language — ideally something that hints at the transformation, not just the topic.
- Acronyms work well when the letters map to real steps (not forced to fit a cute word).
- A metaphor tied to the expert's actual language is stronger than a generic framework name borrowed from the category.

**Examples of the naming pattern** (for inspiration on structure, not for reuse as-is):
- The Visibility-to-Value Framework
- The Authority Alignment Method
- The Story-to-Sales System
- The Client Conversion Pathway
- The Expert Edge Method
- The Message-to-Market Framework

## Competitive Edge Messaging Formula

> I help **[audience]** solve **[specific problem]** so they can achieve **[specific transformation]** through my unique **[framework/process/approach]**.

Work through each slot with the user — vague answers in any slot make the whole statement forgettable.

### Audience
Identify who they serve, what industry, what stage of business or leadership, and what role/identity/problem-state defines them. Push past broad categories.
- Weak: "entrepreneurs"
- Stronger: "emerging speakers," "corporate leaders," "women entrepreneurs," "healthcare executives," "consultants monetizing their expertise," "small business owners trying to clarify their message"

### Specific Problem
Identify the problem the audience actively *feels* — in their language, not the expert's professional jargon.
- Examples: "struggling to explain their value," "losing trust during conflict," "having expertise but no clear offer," "getting overlooked for speaking opportunities," "feeling invisible in the marketplace," "failing to turn their story into a clear brand message"

### Specific Transformation
Identify the desired result — concrete enough to picture, not just aspirational.
- Examples: "become more visible, trusted, and referable," "communicate with confidence and authority," "turn disagreement into better decisions," "build a signature offer from their expertise," "sell their ideas without sounding salesy," "create a message people remember and repeat"

### Unique Process/Framework/Approach
The named method that creates the transformation — this is the signature framework from above, dropped into the formula.
- Examples: "the Story-to-Sales Framework," "the Competitive Edge Messaging Method," "the Signature Story System," "the Authority Story Framework," "the Expert Edge Process"

## Full Story Bank Output Structure

When building a complete story bank (not a single story), organize the deliverable as:

1. **Expert Positioning Statement** — the competitive edge formula, filled in
2. **Signature Framework Name** — proposed name(s)
3. **Framework Description** — "This framework helps [audience] move from [current problem/state] to [desired transformation/result] by guiding them through [core steps/phases]."
4. **The 4 Stories** — origin, credibility, transformation, client — each with its draft and a one-line note on why it matters (see `references/four-stories.md` for the marketplace-message framing for each)
5. **Story Placement Guide** — which story goes where, pulled from the "Best used for" lists in `references/four-stories.md`
