# Messaging formulas and structures

## Competitive Edge Messaging Formula (positioning statement)

> I help [specific audience] solve [specific problem] so they can achieve [specific transformation] through [unique process, framework, or approach].

Example:
"I help business owners clarify their message and position their expertise so they can become easier to understand, easier to trust, and easier to buy from."

## Brand Story Formula

Use this structure to help someone tell their story strategically, not just chronologically:

1. **Before** — What problem, frustration, or gap did they see?
2. **Turning Point** — What changed their perspective?
3. **Discovery** — What did they learn, build, or create?
4. **Credibility** — What experience proves their expertise?
5. **Mission** — Why does this work matter now?
6. **Transformation** — How do they help others create a different outcome?

## Offer Messaging Formula

When describing an offer, include:
- The audience
- The problem
- The promise
- The process
- The results
- The transformation
- The reason to act now
- The next step

Example structure:
"This program helps [audience] move from [current struggle] to [desired outcome] by guiding them through [process]. By the end, they'll have [tangible result], [intangible result], and [strategic benefit]."

## Thought Leadership Structure

Strong thought leadership takes a clear position rather than restating consensus. It should:
- Challenge a common assumption
- Name a problem the audience recognizes
- Offer a new way to think about the issue
- Connect the idea to business outcomes
- Lead back to the person's expertise or offer

Structure:
1. **Common belief** — What does the market usually think?
2. **Your perspective** — What do you believe instead?
3. **Why it matters** — What's at stake?
4. **Application** — What should the audience do differently?
5. **Invitation** — What conversation or action should come next?

## Call-to-action patterns

CTAs should sound natural and aligned with the brand's voice, not generic. Examples of the register to aim for (adapt language, don't reuse verbatim if the user has their own voice already established):
- Naming what clarity would unlock ("Let's clarify the message your market is waiting to hear.")
- Naming the gap directly ("If your audience can't explain what you do, your message needs more clarity.")
- Reframing the sales problem as a communication problem ("Your next client may not need more convincing — they may need clearer communication.")
