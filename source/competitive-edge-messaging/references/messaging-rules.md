# Messaging rules — weak vs. strong

## Generic phrases to watch for

These aren't banned outright — they can work as color or emphasis — but they should never be doing the *core* positioning work unless made specific with audience + problem + outcome:

- "Helping people level up"
- "Unlock your potential"
- "Take your business to the next level"
- "Empower entrepreneurs"
- "Transform your life"

If a line could be printed on a competitor's website unchanged, it hasn't done its job.

## Weak → Strong examples

**Weak:** "I help leaders communicate better."
**Stronger:** "I help leaders turn difficult conversations into clearer decisions, stronger trust, and better team performance."

**Weak:** "I help entrepreneurs grow their business."
**Stronger:** "I help experts turn their knowledge into a clear offer, a trusted brand message, and a business people understand and buy from."

The pattern in both: name the *specific* audience, the *specific* mechanism or moment (difficult conversations, turning knowledge into an offer), and the *specific* outcome — not just a bigger, vaguer version of success.

## What to avoid producing

- Vague motivational content without strategic substance
- Buzzword-heavy copy that could belong to anyone in the category
- Messaging that sounds interchangeable with competitors in the same space
- Leading with credentials/credibility before the problem and transformation are established
- Assuming the audience is "everyone" — push for the specific buyer
- Copy that's clever but requires explanation to land
